Our story

A hardware shop, a second-hand espresso machine, and a lot of stubbornness

How a narrow storefront on Valencia became the place half the block starts its morning.

2016 — today

We were never meant to be a chain

Elena and Marcus took over the lease on 1247 Valencia in the spring of 2016. The space had been a hardware store for thirty-one years, and it still had the original tin ceiling under four layers of paint. It took them six weekends to find it again.

They opened that August with one refurbished La Marzocco, a borrowed grinder, and a hand-painted sign that's still hanging over the pass. The first week they served forty-one customers. Most of them came back.

A decade on, we've added a proper pastry kitchen and about twenty more seats — but the machine is the same one, and so is the answer when someone asks what our secret is: buy carefully, dial it in every morning, and don't rush anybody out the door.

What we care about

Three things we won't cut corners on

Where the coffee comes from

We buy small lots through Bay Area roasters who publish what they pay growers. If we can't find out where a coffee came from, we don't put it on the bar.

How we treat the team

Everyone here is paid above the city minimum, tips are pooled and shared with the kitchen, and the schedule goes out three weeks ahead so people can plan a life.

Being a neighbour

Day-old pastries go to the shelter on Capp Street. The back wall is for local artists. And the bulletin board by the door still works better than any app.

The room

Built for staying, not just grabbing

Thirty-two seats, including the long communal table Marcus built out of the old hardware-store shelving. Outlets under every table along the north wall, Wi-Fi that holds up on a video call, and no time limit on your cup.

Mornings are busy and loud in a good way. Afternoons go quiet — that's when the laptops and the crossword people take over. Everyone gets the room they need.
